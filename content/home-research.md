# Homepage evidence

The homepage uses one directly relevant usability finding. It makes no quantitative conversion, abandonment or client-outcome claim.

Primary publication: [Seckler et al., CHI 2014](https://research.google/pubs/designing-usable-web-forms-empirical-evaluation-of-web-form-improvement-guidelines/), pp. 1275–1284. DOI: 10.1145/2556288.2557265. [The authors' account, July 14, 2014](https://research.google/blog/simple-is-better-making-your-web-forms-easy-to-use-pays-off/), confirms the three real-company forms and experimental setting. Both sources were checked for this revision.

The controlled eye-tracking experiment involved 65 participants. Researchers applied 20 usability guidelines to three forms of differing original quality, then compared the original and improved versions. The improved forms took less time to complete and had more successful first-attempt submissions. Other measures included eye movements and satisfaction.

The homepage mentions 65 people inline as the study participant count; the standalone number display has been removed. It is not a percentage improvement. The section states the finding in plain language. Visible attribution and the source disclosure have been removed at the user's request. The original source, date, method and limitations remain documented here for verification. This laboratory experiment did not measure natural form abandonment, coaching enquiries or client outcomes.

The previous choice-overload and healthcare-reminder material is removed from the homepage. It distracted from the business explanation and required too much contextual qualification for this concise section.
