A first coaching conversation is a chance for both people to understand whether working together makes sense. A simple structure can help you prepare while leaving room to listen.

This is an example framework. Adapt it to your coaching practice, the person and the purpose of the conversation.

## Begin with a shared understanding

Briefly explain the purpose of the conversation and the time available. Ask what would make the discussion useful for the prospective client. If they shared information in advance, acknowledge it so they do not have to start again from the beginning.

## Understand the context

Use open questions to explore why the person is considering coaching now. For example:

- What has prompted you to look into coaching?
- Which situation or decision is most on your mind?
- What support have you already tried?
- What would you like to understand by the end of this conversation?

Follow their answers rather than working through a fixed checklist. Avoid making assumptions based only on their role or job title.

## Explore what a useful engagement could address

Discuss what the person hopes would be different and how they might recognise progress. Keep the discussion grounded in their circumstances. Be clear about what coaching can address within your practice and where another kind of support may be more suitable.

## Explain your approach where it is relevant

Describe how you work in relation to what you have heard. Practical information about the format, responsibilities and beginning of an engagement can be more helpful than an extended explanation of a method.

If you offer several services, explain which option appears relevant and why. Leave space for questions and for the person to consider the fit.

## Agree an appropriate next step

The outcome may be a proposal, further information, an introduction to another professional or a decision not to continue. Make the next step and responsibilities clear. Discuss commercial terms transparently when considering an actual engagement.

## Follow up with context

A useful follow-up reflects the conversation: the situation discussed, the proposed next step and any information you agreed to share. Avoid adding urgency that does not belong to the person's circumstances.

The experience before the consultation matters too. Review your [booking journey](/learn/coaching-booking-page-best-practices) and preparation messages so the conversation begins with the right context.
