title: "What a Coaching Landing Page Needs to Make Clear"
date: "2026-06-03"
updated: "2026-11-16"
excerpt: "A considered review of your coaching offer, service routes, credibility and next step, without treating every practice as the same business."
---

A person visiting your coaching website may already be interested. They might have heard you speak, read your newsletter or received your name from someone they trust. Your page needs to help them understand whether working with you is relevant.

## Make the situation recognisable

A broad promise such as “reach your potential” can mean many things. Describe the people you work with and the situations they bring to coaching. Use language your clients would recognise, without implying that every visitor has the same problem.

For executive coaching, the context might be a change in leadership responsibility, a difficult decision or a period of organisational change. Use the examples that belong to your actual practice.

## Explain the offer in practical terms

Give visitors enough context to evaluate the work. Explain what the coaching involves, who it is suitable for and how the relationship begins. Avoid relying on a method name that a prospective client has never heard before.

If you offer several services, help people find the appropriate route. Individual coaching and organisational work may have different buyers and questions. There is no universal requirement to remove every service except one.

## Put relevant credibility where it helps

Credentials, experience and approved client evidence should support the offer being considered. Make them easy to find and understandable to the audience. Use testimonials and examples only with the appropriate permission, and provide enough context to interpret them fairly.

Avoid unsupported performance claims. A strong client example can show the nature of your work without promising the same result to every future client.

## Give the next step a purpose

An invitation such as “explore executive coaching” can be more informative than a general “get in touch” button. Explain what will happen after the click: an enquiry, a few fit questions, a booking or an email response.

Keep other useful actions in proportion. A newsletter may be valuable for someone still exploring, while a consultation may suit someone ready to discuss the work. Make the relationship between those routes clear.

## Read the page on a phone

Check the actual page at a narrow screen width. The headline, service descriptions, evidence and next step should remain readable. Check that menus open, fields are easy to use, and the booking or contact experience works beyond the first page.

## Ask someone to explain it back

Invite a person who resembles your intended client to read the page. Ask what they think you offer, who it is for and what they would do next. Their explanation can reveal gaps that are difficult to notice when you already know the business.

Use that feedback alongside the [enquiry journey template](/learn/coaching-funnel-template) to decide what deserves attention first.
