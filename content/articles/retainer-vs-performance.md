A proposal should help you understand the work you are considering. For an established coaching business, the most useful starting point is the experience between someone becoming interested and having a qualified conversation with you.

Discuss commercial terms directly alongside the proposed project scope. The considerations below help you assess the work itself.

## Look for an understanding of your business

Does the proposal identify the audience, the offer and the sources of existing interest? An executive coach working with individual leaders and organisational buyers may need different routes for each. A business coach with one core offer may benefit from a more focused structure.

## Ask what the recommendations are based on

Useful recommendations distinguish between an observation, an interpretation and an idea to test. A confusing service label is something you can see. Its effect on enquiries may require further evidence.

Be cautious about claims that go beyond what the available information can support. A considered proposal can acknowledge uncertainty and still recommend a sensible next step.

## Check that the whole experience is considered

The landing page, enquiry or booking process, and follow-up need to work together. Ask whether the proposed scope includes the steps that matter in your business, and which existing tools will be retained.

## Understand the handover

Clarify how the finished work is managed, who can update it, which accounts you need and what support is included. Agree who will approve the content and any third-party integrations before launch.

## Compare complete proposals

Review the thinking, quality of execution, deliverables, responsibilities, commercial terms and after-launch review together. Ask for clarification where a term or responsibility is unclear.

A proposal is most useful when you can explain, in plain language, what is being built and what it is intended to improve.
