Someone hears about your coaching through a referral, a LinkedIn post or a speaking engagement. They visit your website to find out more. What happens next?

This template helps you map that experience. It is a starting point for review, rather than a rule that every coaching business needs the same funnel.

## Start with the interest you already have

Write down where people discover your work and which page they reach. Different sources can bring different levels of context. A personal referral may arrive knowing your name and offer. Someone reading an article may need more explanation.

Where possible, look at actual enquiries and ask new clients how they found you. Note where you have evidence and where you are making an assumption.

## The landing page: help someone recognise the fit

Your page should make it reasonably easy to answer:

- Who is this coaching for?
- What situations or decisions does it help with?
- Which service is relevant to me?
- What does working together involve?
- Why should I trust this coach?
- What is the appropriate next step?

For a single-offer practice, a focused page may be enough. For a coach serving individual leaders and organisations, separate routes can make the offer clearer. Relevant navigation can help visitors find what they need.

## The enquiry or booking: explain the next step

Choose a next step that fits your work. It might be a short enquiry, an asynchronous fit check or a consultation booking.

Explain who it is for, what information you need and what happens afterwards. Only ask questions that help you understand the person or prepare for the conversation. If you use scheduling software, check the experience on a phone as well as a desktop.

## The follow-up: continue the context

A confirmation should make it clear that the request or booking was received. Where useful, include preparation, meeting details, a reminder and a way to reschedule.

If someone needs more time, any follow-up should have a relevant purpose. Avoid sending a long sequence simply because the software allows it.

## Put the steps on one page

Use a simple table for your review:

| Step | What the visitor needs | What to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Recognition and context | Source, first headline, relevant service |
| Evaluation | Understanding and confidence | Offer explanation, approved evidence, practical details |
| Enquiry | A clear, appropriate next step | Invitation, questions, scheduling or contact process |
| Preparation | Reassurance and useful context | Confirmation, preparation and reminders |

## Review what you can observe

Use counts from the same period and source when comparing visits, enquiries and bookings. Consider the quality of conversations as well as their number. Small samples can be noisy, so combine the numbers with direct feedback.
